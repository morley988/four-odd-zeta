The proved result

Assume ζ(7), ζ(9), ζ(11), and ζ(13) are rational. For every sufficiently large natural number n, there is a nonzero rational q_n equal to a selected minor of the actual analytic-coordinate zeta matrix, and

    log |q_n| ≥ −(288/25)(100n)² = −11.52(100n)².

The minor has K_n = floor(361n/2) − 3 rows and columns for n ≥ 1. Its rows and columns are injectively selected from the same matrix used by the existing analytic upper-bound theorem. No change of determinant or unproved coordinate identification is inserted here.

The conditional theorem is
  OddZeta.FourValue.ArithmeticCertificate.global_arithmetic_lower_of_certificate
in FourValueGlobalArithmeticLower.lean. Its kernel axiom set is exactly
  propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

The released wrapper is
  OddZeta.FourValue.ArithmeticCertificate.global_arithmetic_lower
in FourValueGlobalArithmeticComplete.lean. It supplies the external finite certificate and therefore also depends on sorryAx.

The previous analytic upper rate is −577853339/50000000 = −11.55706678. The proved arithmetic lower rate leaves the strict separation

    −11.52 − (−11.55706678) = 1853339/50000000 = 0.03706678.

The result is eventual: no explicit numerical cutoff N is claimed. The cutoff can depend on the fixed denominators under the hypothetical rationality assumption and on the finite certificate's error constants.

What was checked independently

arithmetic_cells.txt has 171,504 rows. Each row contains four exact quantities:
  lower u endpoint, upper u endpoint, integer optimization parameter, claimed rational lower value.
Here u = p/n. The cells cover (1/20, 361] with exact adjacency and no gaps.

verify_arithmetic.py uses python-flint 0.9.0 for arbitrary-precision exact rational arithmetic. It reconstructs 3,810,331 residue boxes, including boxes with zero capacity, checks the rational corner bounds, normalization and capacity conditions, and computes the finite integer optimization penalties. No floating-point comparison is used.

For each box, the integer cost is either m² + a m or max(m² + a m, ν m). The maximum of price*m minus that cost on 0 ≤ m ≤ capacity is attained at an endpoint, an integer nearest the quadratic vertex, or the integer kink ν−a. The checker evaluates those candidates. It does not enumerate every integer up to every capacity.

Each cell-integral contribution is rounded DOWN at 10^−8. The verified lower bound for the total integral is

    −1434459186663/12500000 = −114756.73493304,

which is greater than the finite certificate's required −114800. After division by 10000 and subtraction of the small-prime allowance 361/10000, the verified normalized lower estimate is

    −1438971686663/125000000000 = −11.511773493304.

The check passed all 171,504 cells in 676.93 seconds with four worker processes, while other restoration/build work was running. The maximum reconstructed box penalty was 52,200,625. Three negative controls were rejected: an impossible lower value, an out-of-domain optimization parameter, and a reversed interval. The program rejects Python's -O mode, which would disable assertion checks.

The mathematical assembly

Lean proves the transitions from rational corner bounds to integer floor bounds; from those bounds to local corrected-coordinate determinant valuations; the finite sum over residue classes, including all boundary classes; the selected factorial contribution; the large-prime integral tail; and the rational product-formula inequality.

Writing θ and ψ for the Chebyshev functions, the resulting lower bound is a finite weighted sum of
  n [θ(h_i n) − θ(l_i n)],
minus a fixed certificate error times θ(361n), a denominator term K_n log Q, and
  40 K_n [ψ(800n) − θ(800n) + θ(n/20)].

The prime number theorem, K_n/n → 361/2, and the fixed nature of the error constants give, after division by (100n)², a limiting lower rate at least
  −114800/10000 − 361/10000 = −11.5161.
The strict slack between −11.5161 and −11.52 yields the stated eventual bound.
