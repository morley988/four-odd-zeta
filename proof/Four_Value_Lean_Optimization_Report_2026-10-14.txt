IMPLEMENTATION AND FORMAL SOUNDNESS
FourValueFastInterval.lean:
- Interval endpoints are integer numerators over a shared positive scale.
- Quotient bounds prove downward division and upward ceiling are sound.
- Signed addition, negation, division, and general multiplication are proved.
- A conditional specialization uses only two endpoint products when both
  intervals have nonnegative lower endpoints; the general case remains valid.
- Both the power recurrence and Horner recurrence have proved polynomial
  containment. Rational normalization is absent from their inner loops.
- Conversion back to the established rational interval interface is proved.

FourValueFastEvaluator.lean:
- Reuses the established log/atan remainder bounds with the new backend.
- Logarithms use the verified Horner recurrence; arctangents use the verified
  power recurrence. Both full evaluators retain containment theorems.
- The earlier recursive logarithm frontend is retained for comparison.

FourValueFastReduction.lean:
- Collects the power-of-two exponent before evaluating the logarithms.
- Encloses log(2) once per input, with a signed scalar multiple, instead of
  adding its interval at every range-reduction step.
- The recommended logarithm API is Fast.reducedLogInterval, with theorem
  reducedLogInterval_contains. Fast.atanInterval is the arctangent API.
- Failed reduction returns none; it never supplies an uncertified interval.

TEST SCOPE AND LIMITATIONS
The simple series timing checks nonnegativity of a computed lower endpoint.
The stronger tests check both rational endpoints and their width. Generic
containment theorems separately establish that the true transcendental value
lies inside every returned enclosure. The 1,000-log arguments are (k+1)/37;
the 1,000-atan arguments are (k-500)/13, exercising negative arguments,
quarter-turn and reciprocal reduction. A verified Machin pi enclosure from
Round 23 supplies the pi endpoints.

A first full-test harness failed to synthesize a Decidable instance for a
proposition-valued Option match. It was replaced by an explicit Boolean
Option.any check and then passed. This was an elaboration issue, not a
failed numerical inequality. Both logs are retained for transparency.

The full concrete GridCertificate 50000 is still uninstantiated. Remaining
work is to connect the concrete G/K arrays, exact distance bounds and weighted
knot inequalities to the optimized evaluator, certify the resulting finite
checks in manageable batches, and apply finite_certificate_of_grid. Whole-
certificate runtime has not yet been measured; the 90x figure must not be
applied blindly to that workload. Precision escalation may still be needed
for endpoint inequalities unusually close to their stored ceilings.
Global arithmetic lower bounds and the final contradiction also remain.
No unconditional four-value irrationality theorem is claimed.
