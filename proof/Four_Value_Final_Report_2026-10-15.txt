CONCLUSION
At least one of zeta(7), zeta(9), zeta(11), zeta(13) is irrational.
The main supplied-certificate theorem is:
  OddZeta.FourValue.irrational_zeta7_or_zeta9_or_zeta11_or_zeta13
in lean/FourValueFinal.lean. It uses Mathlib's Irrational predicate and the real
parts of Mathlib's riemannZeta values. The proved realZeta_cast and
realZeta_eq_re lemmas identify these with the ordinary real zeta series at
integers greater than one; taking real parts does not change these values.

THE FINAL COMPARISON
Assume all four values are rational. The arithmetic theorem supplies, for all
sufficiently large n, a nonzero rational q_n equal to a selected minor of the
actual analytic-coordinate matrix, with
  log |q_n| >= -(288/25)(100n)^2 = -11.52(100n)^2.
The analytic theorem applies to every minor of exactly the same size, including
this one, and gives
  |q_n| <= exp(-(577853339/50000000)(100n)^2 + E_n),
where E_n/n^2 tends to zero. For all sufficiently large n, E_n < n^2.
Since q_n is nonzero, taking logarithms is legitimate. The lower and upper
bounds would imply
  370.6678 n^2 <= E_n < n^2,
which is impossible for n >= 1. The normalized rate gap is exactly
  1853339/50000000 = 0.03706678.
There is no additional estimate, numerical assumption, or explicit cutoff
needed in this final comparison. The proof proves existence of a sufficiently
large cutoff, not its numerical value.
