WHAT THE ADAPTER DOES
The old physical rational test evaluates P(4x^2). Define the corrected test
by evaluating P(x^2) instead. Lean proves that this equals the old test
applied to analyticCoordinate P = P(X/4), after exact cancellation.
Under the rationality assumption for the four classical zeta values, the
corrected rational matrix maps entry-by-entry to analyticZetaMatrix n.
The determinant equality holds for EVERY selected row/column pair, not only
for a newly selected arithmetic witness. The previously proved analytic
nonzero minor therefore has a nonzero rational representative q with these
corrected arithmetic bounds.

LOCAL BOUNDS PRESERVED

THE PRIME 2 AND THE MAIN RATE
The potentially expensive coefficientwise substitution X/4 is avoided in
the valuation proof. Instead, Lean uses the exact factorization, for i>=1,
  E_i(x^2) = 2/(2i)! * x * product(t=-i+1,...,i-1)(x-t).
Consecutive integer root counts cancel the factorial valuations. The same
scaled Laurent bound -3 floor(log_p(800n)) holds for each basis factor,
including at p=2. Combining both basis factors, the normalized weight,
and harmonic terms gives, with the fixed pencil denominator Q:
  entries: PadicLower p (-40 floor(log_p(800n)) - v_p(Q));
  k-minors: PadicLower p (-k(40 floor(log_p(800n)) + v_p(Q))).
This includes primes dividing Q.

For the nonzero selected minor k=m+1-4, Lean additionally proves that
  k(40 floor(log_2(800n)) + v_2(Q)) log(2) / n^2 -> 0.
Thus the coordinate adapter adds NO quadratic main-rate penalty at 2.
The large-prime constants and capacities also remain unchanged. This
removes the coordinate mismatch; it does not establish the remaining global
prime-sum lower bound or the numerical certificate inequalities.
