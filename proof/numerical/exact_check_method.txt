INDEPENDENT NUMERICAL CERTIFICATE CHECK — ROUND 22

Scope
The checker uses Python unbounded integers and fractions. NumPy only loads the
saved integer arrays: every arithmetic value read from them is converted to a
Python integer. No floating-point or transcendental-library computation is
used for its enclosure decisions. This checker is NOT a Lean kernel proof.

Dyadic enclosures
All interval endpoints are integers in units of 2^-160. Fraction conversion,
addition, subtraction and rational scalar multiplication round outward. The
functions are represented by the following classical series with explicit
remainder bounds.

Logarithm
Scale the positive rational argument as x=2^e*u, 1<=u<2. Put
t=(u-1)/(u+1), so 0<=t<=1/3. Then
 log u=2 sum_{j=0}^{47} t^(2j+1)/(2j+1)+R,
 0<=R<=2/(97*3^97*(1-1/9)).
Compute log2 using the same formula at u=2. The range reduction is exact.
All powers and polynomial summands are rounded downward. If T=floor(2^160*t)
and T2=floor(T*T/2^160), the computed (2j+1)-st power underestimates the exact
power by at most (4j+1) units. This follows inductively from the initial
one-unit error, a three-unit error bound for T2, and at most one
new rounding unit per power multiplication. Division by 2j+1 and
rounding contributes at most one further unit. Thus the total error in twice
the finite sum is at most 2 sum_{j<N}((4j+1)/(2j+1)+1), which is <=4N^2 for
N=48. The checker adds this generous integer error allowance and the upward
rounded geometric remainder. Signed scaling of log2 is outward rounded.

Arctangent and pi
For 0<=t<=1/2 use 72 terms of the alternating arctangent series. The absolute
remainder is at most 1/(145*2^145). The same power-error argument applies;
4*72^2 units bound the accumulated absolute rounding error. Negative arguments
use oddness. Arguments in (1/2,1] use atan(t)=pi/4+atan((t-1)/(t+1)); arguments
above 1 use atan(t)=pi/2-atan(1/t). Pi is enclosed by Machin's identity
 pi=16atan(1/5)-4atan(1/239).

Analytic coverage
The grid covers [0,5]; the tail formula covers [5,infinity). Passage from
endpoint data to full cells additionally uses the derivative bound and
quadratic interpolation inequality stated in the recovered manuscript.
Passage from knot bounds to intermediate degrees uses linear interpolation
of the masses s*w(s), not linear interpolation of w(s). This file records
the numerical proof method; it does not assert these steps have all been
formalized in Lean. The formalization status is recorded separately.

Justification of full-cell coverage for the weight potential
Write c=1+(3/5)y, and H_a=J(c+a,y)-J(c-a,y). For y>0,
 H_a'=(3/10)log(Q_plus/Q_minus)+atan((c+a)/y)-atan((c-a)/y),
where Q_plus=(c+a)^2+y^2 and Q_minus=(c-a)^2+y^2.
For a=1 both terms are nonnegative. For every denominator endpoint 0<a<1,
 (1+a)^2 Q_minus-(1-a)^2 Q_plus
   =4a*((c-1)*(c-a^2)+y^2)>=0.
Consequently H_a' <= (3/5)log((1+a)/(1-a))+pi/2: the arctangent difference
lies between zero and pi/2. It follows that (G-2pi*y)' >= -L, where L is the
positive constant computed and enclosed by the checker. Integrate this
inequality from y to the right cell endpoint h. Since 0<h-y<=1/10000,
 G(y)-2pi*y <= G(h)-2pi*h+L/10000.
The first cell causes no derivative-at-zero issue: the formal target requires
y>0, and the comparison interval is [y,h], wholly inside the smooth domain.

Justification of the external sharp tail constant
For |z|>1 the symmetric log integral is 2a log|z| plus an absolutely convergent
even-power correction, whose absolute value is at most
 a^3/(3*(|z|^2-1)) for 0<=a<=1. Summing the numerator and denominator
corrections gives D=(5+sum a_i^3)/3. The two truncated distance logarithms
for any center are at most 2log(|z|^2+R), with R=14697/5000. Since s<=C,
4s-2*beta<=0. Using log(1+t)<=t and |z|>=y>=5 gives
 S+D/24+2*C*R/25-10*pi-2*pi*(y-5).
The checker independently encloses the constant term by -33.82285507.
The new Lean tail proof uses a weaker direct norm bound and does not depend
on this series expansion or on the sharp constant.
